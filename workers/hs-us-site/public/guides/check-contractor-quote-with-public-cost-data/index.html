What public data can and cannot do

No government source publishes the price of replacing a roof. Public sources publish the parts a price is made of: wages, import values, trade margins, markup rules and permit lists. So the check works line by line, and only some lines can be checked. A quote that stays a single number cannot be checked at all.

The steps below use an Austin, TX roof as the worked case, because those numbers are dated and sourced on this site. The same steps work for any U.S. quote; the local wage changes with the metro area.

Step 1: get the quote itemized

Ask for crew size and days behind the labor line, squares and product names behind the materials, and the jurisdiction behind the permit. The Federal Trade Commission advises written estimates that describe the work, the materials, the completion date and the price (FTC). If you are still collecting quotes, give every contractor the same page first; see what a scope of work should include.

Step 2: labor against BLS wages and a DOT loading

The BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics give each trade's wage by metro area (BLS OEWS). For roofers in the Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos metro, the May 2025 mean is $24.08 and the 90th percentile $33.24. A contractor pays taxes and insurance on top of the wage, and TxDOT Item 9 allows 25% labor markup plus 55% for insurance and taxes.

That is a loading of 1.80, so a loaded crew hour is $24.08 x 1.80 = $43.34 to $33.24 x 1.80 = $59.83. Multiply by the crew hours on the quote: three roofers for three eight-hour days is 72 hours, or $3,121 to $4,308. Other trades use the same arithmetic; see what construction trades earn per hour in Austin.

The 1.80 loading is a Texas public-works rule; outside Texas, our reports show the wage without a loading rather than borrow it. The wage is a reference, not a billing rate: a labor line well above the 90th percentile figure needs an explanation.

Step 3: materials against the Census import chain

There is no public retail price list for shingles, but there is a public record of what they cost when they enter the country. Imports of asphalt roofing (HS 6807.90.0010) show a landed duty-paid value of $0.4667 per kilogram for January to July 2026 (Census IMDB 2026-07). The Census Annual Integrated Economic Survey gives roofing wholesalers (NAICS 423330) a gross margin of 28.35% (Census AIES 2024). State DOT rules allow a contractor markup on materials of 15% to 25% (Caltrans 15%, FDOT 17.5%, TxDOT 25%).

Divide $0.4667 by 0.7165 (one minus the 28.35% margin) and multiply by 1.15 to 1.25: $0.749 to $0.814 per kilogram. Architectural shingles often weigh about 240 pounds, or 108.86 kilograms, per square, which gives about $82 to $89 per square. The full chain is in what a roof replacement costs in Austin.

This is a floor, not a price: freight to your street, brand premium and any extra distribution tier sit on top. A shingle line above the floor is expected; a line far above it should come with a reason you can check. Material prices move: the BLS construction materials index rose 10.1% in the year to August 2026 (BLS PPI via FRED). That moves the materials lines, not labor or permits; see when a contractor says material prices went up.

Step 4: overhead against state DOT markups

The TxDOT loading in step 2 already covers labor markup, insurance and taxes. If a quote adds an overhead and profit line to labor already at or above the loaded rate, the same costs are counted twice. That is a question, not an error: ask what the overhead line covers that the labor rate does not. The public markup rules are in overhead and profit on a contractor quote.

Step 5: the permit against the city's rules

Check whether the city requires a permit for the work at all. Inside Austin city limits, asphalt shingles replacing asphalt shingles on one and two family homes up to three stories are exempt (City of Austin). The exception is the Wildland-Urban Interface area when 50% or more of the roofing is replaced. Where a permit is required, the fee is on the city's schedule and should pass through at cost with a receipt. The full exemption text is in do I need a permit to replace my roof in Austin.

Step 6: lines with no public benchmark

Tear-off and disposal, underlayment, flashing and vents have no public reference. For those, the check is a quantity: squares and layers for the tear-off, product names for the accessories. The squares on the tear-off should match the squares on the shingle line. We do not invent a number where there is none.

Step 7: redo the arithmetic from the open dataset

The wage, import, margin and markup figures above are published as the United States Construction Cost Database (USCCDB) v1.0. It is open under CC BY 4.0 at doi:10.5281/zenodo.22979157, so anyone can check the numbers we print, including the contractor. Each figure on this page also links to the government record it comes from.

What to send the contractor

  1. Crew size and days behind the labor line.
  2. Squares, product name and waste percentage behind the shingle line.
  3. Squares and layers behind the tear-off.
  4. What the overhead line covers that the labor rate does not.
  5. The permit jurisdiction, and the receipt if a permit is pulled.

Questions

Can public data tell me what my job should cost in total?

No. Government sources publish wages, import values, margins and permit rules, not finished job prices. They can check the parts of a quote you already have.

What if my quote is from outside Texas?

The steps work for any U.S. quote. The BLS publishes wages for every metro area; the 1.80 loading is a Texas rule, so elsewhere the wage is shown without it.

Where can I download the numbers?
