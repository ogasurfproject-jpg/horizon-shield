Federico,

Thank you for sending it on.

Your reason for replaying one is better than the one my draft had, and I want to be precise about why rather than just agreeing. My draft asserted one call, disclosed by name. It gave no reason. You gave the reason: determinism is "same input twice", not "different inputs once each", so replaying every published call does not improve the determinism reading at all. It buys tool coverage, which is a different measurement wearing the same clothes. That distinction is the kind of thing that only shows up when someone implements the spec rather than reads it. It is in the draft now, in your words, with your name on it.

The coverage ratio is the better half of your message. It does not close cherry-picking and you said so yourself, which is why I trust it. What it does is convert a silent choice into a number: "3 of 33 tools have owner-published samples" is a fact a reader can weigh, where the absence of that line is a fact nobody can see. That is also in the draft, credited.

One thing I have to put on top of it, because it is the same defect one level up.

The denominator comes from the server's own tools/list. That count is also chosen by the party being verified. An operator who wants a flattering ratio has two moves: publish more samples, which is the outcome we want, or list fewer tools, which is the outcome we do not. Nothing in the ratio distinguishes "3 of 33, and here are the 33" from "3 of 4, because 29 were never declared". So the ratio needs its own companion fact, and I think it is the digest of the sorted tool-name set at the time of measurement, published in the verdict. It does not prove the set is complete. Nothing can, from outside. But it makes a shrinking denominator visible across measurements instead of invisible, which is the same trade your ratio makes one level down.

Related, and the reason I am confident this shape is worth arguing about: I shipped it today. The gate went to 0.3.0 this morning. Until this version, the day a free-tier endpoint got measured was sha256(endpoint) mod 7, every input public, so a server could compute its own measurement day and be up one day in seven. And the determinism probe measured whichever tool the server listed first, which the server chooses. Two coordinates, both held by the party being verified. Now both are derived from a salt the gate commits to at the start of each window, bound to a Bitcoin block mined after that salt existed: the subject cannot predict them, and the gate cannot choose them after the fact, because the commitment predates the block. The tool is picked over the lexicographically sorted name set, so reordering steers nothing.

I published the defect before I published the fix, with the arithmetic of the old exploit spelled out, because I had already sent that same arithmetic to an outside operator in a letter this week in the name of transparency. It was an honest disclosure and a working exploit recipe at the same time. Both readings were correct, so the record says both.

Two red teams, on two independent implementations of the same rule: 26 vectors on the gate's, 17 on the reference one. Same discipline you and I have been circling all week, applied to my own instrument.
